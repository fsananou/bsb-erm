\section*{Part I --- Risk Concepts \& ERM Governance \hfill (25 points)}

\subsection*{Question 1 \hfill (12 points)}

\textbf{(a) Risk vs.\ uncertainty (6 pts).}
Explain the difference between \emph{risk} and \emph{uncertainty} in the ERM context.
Your answer must address: (i) what can and cannot be reliably quantified,
and (ii) why scenario analysis complements (rather than replaces) quantitative risk metrics.

\medskip
\textbf{(b) Why silo risk management fails (6 pts).}
Explain why managing risks in silos can increase enterprise-wide vulnerability, even when each
risk category appears well controlled locally.
Support your argument with a concrete example involving at least \emph{two} risk categories
(e.g.\ market + liquidity; credit + funding; operational + reputational).

\subsection*{Question 2 \hfill (13 points)}

Consider the statement:
\begin{quote}
``ERM fails when risk information exists but does not change decisions.''
\end{quote}

Propose an ERM architecture that increases the probability that risk signals will affect decisions.
Your answer must address:
\begin{itemize}
  \item[$\bullet$] the distinction between risk \emph{ownership} and risk \emph{oversight},
  \item[$\bullet$] escalation thresholds and exception governance,
  \item[$\bullet$] why independence of the risk function is necessary but not sufficient.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Part II --- Measurement \& Hedging \hfill (40 points)}

\subsection*{Question 3 --- RAROC and EVA \hfill (10 points)}

A business unit proposes a new portfolio with the following annual figures (in \texteuro{} million):
\[
NI = 28 \qquad EC = 200 \qquad k = 11\%
\]
where $NI$ is net income after expected losses, $EC$ is economic capital allocated at the chosen
confidence level, and $k$ is the firm-wide hurdle rate.
For EVA purposes, assume invested capital equals economic capital.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] Define \textbf{RAROC} and compute it for this portfolio. \hfill (3 pts)
  \item[(b)] Define \textbf{EVA} and compute it. \hfill (3 pts)
  \item[(c)] A manager concludes: ``$\text{RAROC} > k \Rightarrow$ always accept.''
  Give \emph{two} reasons why this rule can be misleading in ERM practice. \hfill (4 pts)
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Question 4 --- Hedging: prospective vs.\ retrospective effectiveness + tail risk \hfill (30 points)}

A firm hedges a monthly exposure using a derivative.
Let $X$ be the unhedged monthly P\&L (\texteuro{}m) and $H$ the monthly P\&L of the hedging instrument.
The hedged position is $Y = X - hH$.
The hedge ratio $h$ is set using normal-period estimates and kept fixed during stress.

\paragraph{Data (monthly, \texteuro{}m)}

\noindent
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Period & $\mathrm{Var}(X)$ & $\mathrm{Var}(H)$ & $\mathrm{Cov}(X,H)$ \\
\midrule
Normal (estimation) & 100 & 64 & 48 \\
Stress (realized)   & 225 & 144 & 12 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\medskip
Hedge effectiveness is defined as:
\[
\text{HE} = 1-\frac{\mathrm{Var}(Y)}{\mathrm{Var}(X)}, \qquad
\mathrm{Var}(Y) = \mathrm{Var}(X) + h^2\,\mathrm{Var}(H) - 2h\,\mathrm{Cov}(X,H)
\]

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] Compute the minimum-variance hedge ratio:
  $h^* = \dfrac{\mathrm{Cov}(X,H)}{\mathrm{Var}(H)}$ using normal-period data. \hfill (4 pts)

  \item[(b)] Using $h = h^*$, compute $\mathrm{Var}(Y)$ and $\text{HE}_{\text{pros}}$ in the normal period. \hfill (5 pts)

  \item[(c)] Keeping $h = h^*$, compute $\mathrm{Var}(Y)$ and $\text{HE}_{\text{retro}}$ using stress-period data. \hfill (5 pts)

  \item[(d)] Interpret the change from $\text{HE}_{\text{pros}}$ to $\text{HE}_{\text{retro}}$.
  Your answer must reference \emph{regime change} and at least one of:
  basis/correlation breakdown, model risk, or liquidity/market functioning. \hfill (4 pts)

  \item[(e)] \textbf{Tail-risk check.} In stress months, the unhedged loss distribution of $X$ is:
  \[
  L_X \in \{0,\; 5,\; 10,\; 25,\; 40\} \quad \text{with probabilities} \quad
  \{0.40,\; 0.25,\; 0.20,\; 0.10,\; 0.05\}
  \]
  (Positive values are losses, in \texteuro{}m.)
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)] Compute $\mathrm{VaR}_{95\%}(L_X)$. \hfill (3 pts)
    \item[(ii)] Compute $\mathrm{ES}_{95\%}(L_X)$ (expected loss conditional on exceeding VaR$_{95\%}$). \hfill (5 pts)
    \item[(iii)] Explain in one or two sentences why $\mathrm{ES}$ is more informative than $\mathrm{VaR}$ for tail-risk management. \hfill (4 pts)
  \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Question 5 --- Cross-industry ERM \hfill (15 points)}

Compare ERM in \emph{two} industries (choose two: financial institutions, energy \& commodities,
non-financial corporations, public sector \& infrastructure).
For each industry, explain in no more than one short paragraph:
\begin{itemize}
  \item[$\bullet$] how industry economics shape ERM priorities,
  \item[$\bullet$] the main instruments used (models/capital, hedging, operational controls, etc.),
  \item[$\bullet$] a typical failure mode specific to that industry.
\end{itemize}
Conclude with one sentence on the key difference between the two industries you selected.

\subsection*{Question 6 --- Case: ``ERM existed, crisis still happened'' \hfill (20 points)}

A large firm has adopted a recognized ERM framework, produces regular risk reports, and uses
quantitative risk metrics. Despite this, it experiences a major crisis driven by a combination
of market stress, liquidity pressure, and delayed escalation.

Diagnose the failure. Your answer must:
\begin{itemize}
  \item[$\bullet$] distinguish \emph{measurement failure} from \emph{governance failure} (with one example of each),
  \item[$\bullet$] explain the role of incentives in delayed escalation,
  \item[$\bullet$] explain why ``more data'' or ``better models'' would not necessarily have prevented the crisis,
  \item[$\bullet$] propose \emph{two} concrete architecture or process changes that would reduce recurrence.
\end{itemize}
